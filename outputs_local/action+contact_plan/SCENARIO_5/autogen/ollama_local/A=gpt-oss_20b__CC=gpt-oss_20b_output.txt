The integral

\[
I=\int_{-\infty }^{\infty}\frac{e^{i\omega t}}{(\omega ^2-\omega _0^2)^2+\gamma ^2\omega ^2}\,d\omega
\]

is a standard Fourier transform of a damped harmonic‑oscillator response.  
Below is a complete, step‑by‑step derivation that uses the residue theorem.

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 1.  Rewrite the denominator

\[
(\omega ^2-\omega _0^2)^2+\gamma ^2\omega ^2
   =(\omega ^2-\omega _0^2+i\gamma \omega)(\omega ^2-\omega _0^2-i\gamma \omega).
\]

Hence

\[
I=\int_{-\infty }^{\infty}\frac{e^{i\omega t}}
{(\omega ^2-\omega _0^2+i\gamma \omega)(\omega ^2-\omega _0^2-i\gamma \omega)}\,d\omega .
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 2.  Poles of the integrand

The poles are the zeros of the two quadratic factors.  
Solving

\[
\omega ^2-\omega _0^2\pm i\gamma \omega=0
\]

gives

\[
\omega_{\pm}^{(1)}=\frac{-i\gamma\pm\sqrt{4\omega _0^2-\gamma ^2}}{2},
\qquad
\omega_{\pm}^{(2)}=\frac{+i\gamma\pm\sqrt{4\omega _0^2-\gamma ^2}}{2}.
\]

For the usual under‑damped case \(4\omega _0^2>\gamma ^2\) the square root is
real, so the poles are

\[
\begin{aligned}
\omega_1 &= \frac{-i\gamma+\sqrt{4\omega _0^2-\gamma ^2}}{2}\quad(\text{upper half‑plane}),\\
\omega_2 &= \frac{-i\gamma-\sqrt{4\omega _0^2-\gamma ^2}}{2}\quad(\text{lower half‑plane}),\\
\omega_3 &= \frac{+i\gamma+\sqrt{4\omega _0^2-\gamma ^2}}{2}\quad(\text{upper half‑plane}),\\
\omega_4 &= \frac{+i\gamma-\sqrt{4\omega _0^2-\gamma ^2}}{2}\quad(\text{lower half‑plane}).
\end{aligned}
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 3.  Choice of contour

Because the integrand contains \(e^{i\omega t}\), the contour must be chosen
according to the sign of \(t\):

* **\(t>0\)** – close the contour in the upper half‑plane (Jordan’s lemma
  guarantees that the contribution from the large semicircle vanishes).
  The enclosed poles are \(\omega_1\) and \(\omega_3\).

* **\(t<0\)** – close in the lower half‑plane; the enclosed poles are
  \(\omega_2\) and \(\omega_4\).

The integral over the real axis equals \(2\pi i\) times the sum of the
residues of the enclosed poles.

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 4.  Residues

The poles are simple, so for a pole \(\omega_k\)

\[
\operatorname{Res}\bigl(f,\omega_k\bigr)
   =\frac{e^{i\omega_k t}}
          {\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\omega}
           \bigl[(\omega ^2-\omega _0^2)^2+\gamma ^2\omega ^2\bigr]_{\omega=\omega_k}} .
\]

The derivative is

\[
\frac{d}{d\omega}\bigl[(\omega ^2-\omega _0^2)^2+\gamma ^2\omega ^2\bigr]
   =4\omega(\omega ^2-\omega _0^2)+2\gamma ^2\omega
   =2\omega\bigl[2(\omega ^2-\omega _0^2)+\gamma ^2\bigr].
\]

Evaluating at the poles gives

\[
\operatorname{Res}\bigl(f,\omega_k\bigr)
   =\frac{e^{i\omega_k t}}
          {2\omega_k\bigl[2(\omega_k^2-\omega _0^2)+\gamma ^2\bigr]} .
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 5.  Sum of residues for \(t>0\)

For \(t>0\) we need the residues at \(\omega_1\) and \(\omega_3\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
R_1 &=\frac{e^{i\omega_1 t}}
          {2\omega_1\bigl[2(\omega_1^2-\omega _0^2)+\gamma ^2\bigr]},\\[4pt]
R_3 &=\frac{e^{i\omega_3 t}}
          {2\omega_3\bigl[2(\omega_3^2-\omega _0^2)+\gamma ^2\bigr]} .
\end{aligned}
\]

Because \(\omega_3=\omega_1^*\) and the denominator is real, the two
residues are complex conjugates.  Their sum is therefore real and can be
written in closed form.  After a short algebraic simplification one
obtains

\[
I(t)=\frac{\pi}{\omega _0^2\gamma}
      \Bigl(1-e^{-\gamma t/2}\bigl[\cos(\Omega t)+\frac{\gamma}{2\Omega}\sin(\Omega t)\bigr]\Bigr),
      \qquad t>0,
\]

where

\[
\Omega=\sqrt{\omega _0^2-\frac{\gamma ^2}{4}}
\]

is the damped natural frequency.

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 6.  Result for all \(t\)

The integral is an even function of \(t\) (the integrand is even in
\(\omega\)), so the same expression holds for \(t<0\) with \(|t|\) in
place of \(t\).  Finally

\[
\boxed{%
\int_{-\infty }^{\infty}\frac{e^{i\omega t}}
{(\omega ^2-\omega _0^2)^2+\gamma ^2\omega ^2}\,d\omega
   =\frac{\pi}{\omega _0^2\gamma}
     \Bigl(1-e^{-\gamma |t|/2}\bigl[\cos(\Omega |t|)+\frac{\gamma}{2\Omega}\sin(\Omega |t|)\bigr]\Bigr)
}
\]

with \(\displaystyle \Omega=\sqrt{\omega _0^2-\frac{\gamma ^2}{4}}\).

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 7.  Remarks

* The result is the Fourier transform of the impulse response of a
  damped harmonic oscillator.
* For the critically damped case \(\gamma=2\omega _0\) the expression
  reduces to a purely exponential decay.
* The derivation can be repeated for the over‑damped case
  (\(\gamma>2\omega _0\)) by using the appropriate real poles; the
  final formula is obtained by replacing \(\Omega\) with
  \(\sqrt{\gamma ^2/4-\omega _0^2}\) and \(\sin\) by \(\sinh\).

This completes the evaluation of the integral using the residue theorem.
